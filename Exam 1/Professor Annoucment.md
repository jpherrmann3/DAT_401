EXAM MATERIAL AND QUESTIONS
Exam 1 covers the material discussed in all the videos from the beginning of the course, up to and including the 10th video in the 3rd module, i.e. up to and including M3.10 CLT; Simulations and Remarks. There will be about 10 questions, all of which are multiple choice questions. This means you either get full credit for the question, or 0 pts. Therefore, there is NO partial credit within a question. Be careful which offered choice you select, as you can lose a lot of points, since each question is about 10-11% of the entire exam. Most of the offered choices are results obtained after making some typical, common mistakes. So, if you get an answer that is offered in the list, that still doesn't mean there is a great chance you got it right, as you may have made a mistake that many students do at some point of the process. So, double-check your answers. If you are done and have more time left, then you should triple-check your answers. And then quadruple-check :-). Again, you lose a lot of points if your answer is wrong (as well as get a lot of points if you are correct). The total score is 100 pts.


STUDY GUIDE

Almost all (if not all) exam questions are similar to some questions discussed in videos, slides, lecture notes and/or homework. So, while you may find some questions harder, there are no surprises or tricky questions. To prepare for the exam, do the following:

- Do the practice exam questions posted in this announcement. You also have the solution file, but don't look at the solution until you do the problems. Or, if you really tried but cannot solve it, it's probably better to first go over the corresponding material in class (videos, slides, lecture notes, similar questions, etc.) making sure you understand how similar questions are done and then tackle the problem again, rather than directly looking into the solution.

- Redo homework questions (including HW2), especially parts regarding math (i.e. not programming)
- Do all (or as many as possible) examples in videos/slides/lectures by yourself.
- Take mini mock-up exam (see below), if not earlier. You can find it if you go to Quizzes --> Practice Quizzes

- Please feel free to hop into my office zoom room for office hours and ask questions. The zoom link and hours are at the beginning of Syllabus.

Respondus Lockdown Browser and Mock-Up Exam


THE EXAM WILL BE PROCTORED!
We use Respondus software for proctoring your exam. This is a system that records from your web camera and screen, prevents using certain programs and web, and runs other proctoring tools. To get comfortable with Respondus, I will set up a mock-up exam, which you are allowed to take multiple times. This is NOT a complete practice exam - it only contains a couple of exam-like questions. Its main purpose is two-fold: to get you know about the system and make you comfortable with it, as well as to give you an idea about what kind of questions and what level of difficulty you might expect.

The proctoring software will make you take a photo, show your ID, scan the room where you are taking the exam, as well as your computer screen. In case the proctoring software does not validate the photo, or ID, or the room conditions, it will ask you to redo it. If you cannot go through the validation after a couple of attempts, it will offer you to contact a Respondus representative for clarification and for solving the problem.
It might ask you to have Chrome browser installed (although I am not so sure about this).

Proctoring software will take the following actions during the entire exam:

- Record Your Webcam
- Record Your Screen
- Record Your Web Traffic
- Disable Copy/Paste
- Disable Printing

Also, the proctoring software is set to the following conditions:

- Require LockDown Browser to view post-exam feedback and results
- Enable Calculator on the toolbar (Scientific Calculator)
