Topics: Among other things, you need to prepare for the following:

- You must know what MLE is and how to find it, both for a parameter of discrete and continuous random variable.

- You must know confidence intervals. Rather than memorizing, start from basic setup and work up using algebra. You should know what to do if the population is normal and sigma is known, as well as when sigma is unknown but sample standard deviation is given. Also, you should know what to do when estimating a proportion.

- You must know what posterior probability and posterior density are; you must know Bayes formula, both discrete and continuous version

- You must know how to find posterior density; you should utilize the fact that it doesn't matter what normalizing constant is; keep track of the functional part only; then, when you simplify the expression, you set the scaling factor such so that the integral below the density and over the entire real line is equal to 1.

- You must know how to set up a hypothesis test (recall that the hypothesis you are trying to show should be Ha - the alternative hypothesis). You must know what type I and type II errors are, what p-value is, etc.

- You must know that critical (a.k.a. rejection region) is a region on the real line so that if your statistic falls into that region, you reject the null hypothesis Ho (and accept the alternative hypothesis Ha)
