# 1.2 Assessing Probability

There are several approaches to assessing/understanding the probability of an uncertain event:

1. classical probability: works for finite samples spaces that contain equally likely outcomes.

$$
P(A) = \frac{|A|}{|S|}
$$

2. frequentist probabilty: relative frequency

$$
P(A) = \frac{\text{number of times }A\text{ occured}}{\text{number of trials}}
$$

3. Bayesian probability (from personal judgement): an individual judgement/opinion/belief about the probability of occurrence.

A die is cast.

2. What is the probability that the die is fair?
3. What is the probability that tomorrow Google stock (GOOG) will open higher than it closes today?
4. What is the probability that tomorrow will rain?
5. What is the probability that the universe started with the Big Bang?

## Axioms of probability:

Intuitively and informally, we would like to think of probability of an event as a measure of certainty or our confidence that the event will happen if we perform experiement where the outcome is not completely predictable/deterministic, and where our event of interest may or may not happen.

We would like the definition of probability to be as broad as possible covering all the situations that are of practical interest including all aforementioned, and meeting all the expectations of classical, frequentist, and bayesian approach.

This is done by thinking of events as subsets of the sample space $S$ and setting up the minimal rules that probability $P$ as a set function should satisfy. These rules/properties are called *axioms*.
