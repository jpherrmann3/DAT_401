# 1.1 Classical Definition of Probability

In general, if a sample space $S$ is finite and consists of equally likely outcomes, the probability of any even $A \subseteq S$ is defined as

$$
P(A) = \frac{\#(A)}{\#(S)} = \frac{\text{number of outcomes in } A}{\text{number of all outcomes in }S} \stackrel{\text{denote}}{=} \frac{|A|}{|S|}
$$

However, this only works if the sample space consists of finitely many outcomes and if all the outcomes are equally likely. There are man other situations we would like to consider, but we do not have this simple setup.

- What about casting a die until 6 appears? Here all we care about is how man ycasts were performed until 6 appeared, and so, the sample space is $S=\{1, 2, 3, \ldots\} = \mathbb{N}$. Or, should we include outcomes that we never get 6 (denote by $\infty$), so that the sample space is $S = \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$? Let A = event that the experiment stops within 3 casts, i.e. $A = \{1, 2, 3\}$. What is $P(A)$? What is $P(\{\infty\})$?
- What about choosing a point from an interval [0, 1]? Here, $S = [0, 1]$. What is $P(\{\frac{1}{2}\})$, the probability of getting the number $\frac{1}{2}$? What is the $P([0, \frac{1}{2}])$, the probability of getting the number in the first half of the interval?
- Does it make sense to assign probability that it will rain tomorrow?
